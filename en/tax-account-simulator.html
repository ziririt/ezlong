What is Tax-Advantaged Account After-Tax Simulator?

The Tax-Advantaged Account After-Tax Simulator quantifies one of the most powerful and most underestimated forces in long-term investing: the compounding advantage of tax-deferred growth. The simulator compares two parallel scenarios — investing through a tax-advantaged account versus a regular taxable brokerage — and shows the after-tax wealth difference over time.

In a tax-advantaged account like a Roth IRA, traditional 401k, or Korean ISA/IRP, your investments grow without annual taxation on gains. In a taxable brokerage account, you pay capital gains tax each year on your returns, reducing the amount available to compound. Over 20-30 years, this tax drag creates a stunning wealth gap.

The simulator supports US account types (Roth IRA, traditional 401k, standard brokerage) and Korean account types (ISA, IRP, pension savings). You can adjust the annual return rate, capital gains tax rate, and investment period to see exactly how the gap grows under your specific circumstances.

Why Use This Tool?

The single most impactful financial decision most investors can make is not picking better stocks — it is making sure their investments are in the right account type. Paying taxes annually on investment gains is one of the biggest wealth destroyers in long-term investing, and it is entirely avoidable for most investors.

Consider: $10,000 growing at 9% annually for 30 years in a tax-advantaged account grows to approximately $132,000. The same investment in a taxable account paying 20% capital gains tax each year grows to only about $88,000. That $44,000 difference — 33% of your final wealth — was consumed entirely by an avoidable annual tax drag. This simulator makes that gap visible and personal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a tax-advantaged investment account?

A tax-advantaged account is a government-sanctioned account type where investments receive special tax treatment. In the US: Roth IRA contributions grow tax-free. Traditional 401k and IRA defer taxes until withdrawal. In Korea: ISA and IRP accounts receive favorable tax treatment on investment gains.

How much does annual tax drag reduce long-term returns?

Annual tax drag of 15-20% on investment returns can reduce final wealth by 20-35% over 30 years. For example, $10,000 at 9% return over 30 years grows to $132,000 tax-deferred but only $88,000 in a taxable account with 20% annual capital gains tax — a $44,000 difference.

What is the Roth IRA advantage?

In a Roth IRA, you contribute after-tax dollars, and all growth and qualified withdrawals are completely tax-free. This is especially powerful for young investors with long time horizons, because compound growth over 30-40 years completely eclipses the initial tax paid on contributions.

What is the 401k advantage?

A traditional 401k allows pre-tax contributions, reducing your taxable income today. Investments grow tax-deferred, and you pay income tax only when you withdraw in retirement — typically at a lower tax rate. Many employers also match contributions, which is essentially free money.

What is the Korean ISA account?

The Korean Individual Savings Account (ISA) allows investors to hold US stocks, domestic stocks, and bonds in a single tax-advantaged account. Gains up to 2 million KRW are tax-exempt, and gains above that threshold are taxed at a reduced 9.9% rate instead of the standard 15.4-22%.

Does this simulator work for Korean investors in US stocks?

Yes. The simulator supports both Korean and US account types. For Korean investors holding US stocks directly, the standard capital gains and dividend tax rates apply. The simulator lets you compare the tax impact across ISA, IRP, pension savings, and direct investment scenarios.

When does the tax-advantaged account benefit become most significant?

The benefit grows exponentially with time. In the first 5 years, the gap may seem small. By year 15, it becomes substantial. By year 30, the difference in after-tax wealth is typically 25-35% of total portfolio value — meaning more than a quarter of your retirement wealth depends on account choice.
