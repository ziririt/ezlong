What is Investment Behavior Self-Assessment?

The Investment Behavior Self-Assessment is an adaptive quiz that diagnoses the psychological patterns most likely to hurt your investment returns. It is not a generic personality test and it does not ask about your age, income, or specific holdings — it asks about your actual, real-world trading habits and scores you across 5 behavior clusters: Emotional Reactions, Trading Behavior, Information Consumption, Portfolio Structure, and Risk Management Basics.

Rather than a fixed list of questions, the quiz is adaptive: your answer to certain gate questions determines which follow-up question you see next. Most people answer between 9 and 15 of the 15 available questions in about 3 to 6 minutes. At the end, you're scored into one of 5 grades — Stable, Cautious, Alert, Risky, or High-Risk — and shown exactly which specific patterns (FOMO reaction, panic-selling history, disposition effect, overtrading, leverage dependence, and more) drove your score.

Nothing is saved anywhere. There's no login, no account, and no data leaves your browser — the entire assessment, scoring, and results screen run client-side.

Why Use This Tool?

Behavioral finance research consistently shows that the average investor significantly underperforms the market — not because they pick bad stocks, but because they make systematically bad timing decisions driven by emotion. DALBAR's annual study shows that the average US equity fund investor earns several percentage points less per year than the funds they actually hold, simply due to buying high and selling low at the wrong times.

Most of those behavioral misfires stay invisible in a rising market — they only surface during the next real correction, and by then it's too late to prepare for them. This assessment exists to surface them now, while markets are calm, so you can decide in advance how you'll actually handle a 20% or 40% drawdown instead of finding out for the first time when it happens.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does this investment behavior assessment actually measure?

It measures behavioral patterns across 5 clusters: emotional reactions (FOMO, panic selling, disposition effect), trading behavior (overtrading, failed market timing, chart dependence), information consumption (news/YouTube overconsumption, chasing macro data, copying star investors), portfolio structure (too many holdings, high-yield covered-call illusions, long-term leveraged-ETF accumulation), and risk management basics (cash buffer, written investment rules). It does not ask about your age, income, or specific holdings.

How many questions are there, and how long does it take?

The assessment is adaptive — your answer to certain questions determines which follow-up question you see next, so most people answer between 9 and 15 of the 15 available questions. It typically takes about 3 to 6 minutes.

What are the 5 risk grades?

Scores of 0-14 points are graded Stable, 15-24 Cautious, 25-35 Alert, 36-50 Risky, and 51 or more High-Risk. The grade reflects how many behavioral misfires were detected and how strongly, not a prediction of future returns.

Is this the same as a risk-tolerance questionnaire from a brokerage?

No. A brokerage risk-tolerance questionnaire typically asks how you'd feel about a hypothetical loss to recommend an asset allocation. This assessment instead looks at your actual past and current trading behavior — FOMO buying, panic selling, leverage use, cash reserves — to flag specific behavioral misfires that are proven to hurt long-term returns.

Why do 3x leveraged ETFs come up in this assessment?

Products like TQQQ and SOXL are subject to volatility decay: if the underlying index falls 50% and then recovers 100%, it's back to breakeven, but a 3x leveraged fund tracking it would be down roughly 150% and mathematically cannot recover the same way. Treating these as a simple long-term buy-and-accumulate holding is one of the specific behavioral misfires this assessment checks for.

Are my answers or results stored anywhere?

No. The assessment runs entirely in your browser. No account is required, and no answers or results are transmitted to or stored on any server.

Is a high-risk grade a bad sign, or normal?

It's common, especially for investors who got started or added leverage during a strong bull market — behavioral misfires that don't show up in rising markets tend to surface only during the next real correction. A higher grade simply means more misfires were detected across the 5 clusters, which is exactly the point of finding out now rather than during the next crash.
