What is the Monte Carlo Portfolio Optimizer?

The Monte Carlo Portfolio Optimizer uses 20 years of real US stock market data (2006-2025) to test thousands of randomly generated portfolio weight combinations and find your optimal asset allocation. Instead of guessing what percentage to put in NVDA versus SPY versus QQQ, the tool tests 1,000 to 5,000 combinations (your choice) and calculates the expected return, volatility, and Sharpe Ratio for every single one, using each asset's real historical mean returns, volatility, and correlations.

You can choose from 44 assets including major US stocks (NVDA, GOOGL, AAPL, MSFT, TSLA, AMZN) and ETFs (SPY, QQQ, VTI, SCHD, TLT) — up to 10 at a time. Out of every combination tested, the tool highlights three portfolios: the Maximum Sharpe Ratio allocation (best risk-adjusted efficiency), the Minimum Volatility allocation (lowest risk), and a portfolio matched to your stated loss tolerance and return target.

The output is a scatter chart plotting every tested combination by risk (x-axis) and return (y-axis) — this is the efficient frontier. You also get a year-by-year return breakdown, an asset correlation matrix, a projected growth chart for your initial investment plus optional monthly DCA, and a side-by-side comparison against simple S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 buy-and-hold.

Why Use This Tool?

Picking a portfolio allocation by gut feeling — "60% SPY, 40% QQQ, seems reasonable" — skips the actual math. Every combination of assets has a specific, calculable relationship between risk and return based on how those assets have historically moved, both individually and relative to each other (correlation). Two portfolios that look similar on paper can have meaningfully different volatility and drawdown profiles.

By testing thousands of random weight combinations against 20 years of real historical data, the optimizer maps out the entire risk/return landscape for your chosen assets — the efficient frontier — so you can see exactly what tradeoff every allocation offers. This lets you ask a better question than "what should I buy?": "given the assets I want to hold, what's the most efficient way to combine them for my risk tolerance?"

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Monte Carlo simulation for portfolio allocation?

A Monte Carlo portfolio simulation randomly generates thousands of different weight combinations across your chosen assets, then calculates the expected return, volatility, and Sharpe Ratio for each one using 20 years of real historical data. Instead of guessing an allocation, you get to see the full risk/return landscape and pick the combination that best fits your goals.

How many assets are available in this optimizer?

The EZLONG Monte Carlo Portfolio Optimizer includes 44 US stocks and ETFs: NVDA, GOOGL, AAPL, MSFT, TSLA, AMZN, SPY, QQQ, VTI, SCHD, TLT, and more. You can select up to 10 at once. All calculations use 20 years of real return data (2006-2025) by default, with 10-year and pre-COVID windows also available.

What is the Sharpe Ratio and why does it matter?

The Sharpe Ratio measures how much return you get per unit of risk (volatility). A higher Sharpe Ratio means better risk-adjusted efficiency. The Maximum Sharpe portfolio gives you the best return-per-risk combination out of every weight tested — not necessarily the highest absolute return, but the most efficient use of risk.

What is the Minimum Volatility portfolio?

The Minimum Volatility portfolio is the specific weight combination, among every one tested, that produces the lowest possible portfolio volatility for your chosen assets. It suits risk-averse investors who prioritize capital preservation over maximum growth.

What are the three recommended portfolios?

After testing thousands of random weight combinations, the tool highlights three: the portfolio matched to your stated risk profile (loss tolerance and return target), the Maximum Sharpe portfolio (best risk-adjusted return), and the Minimum Volatility portfolio (lowest risk). Each comes with its own projected growth, drawdown, and Sharpe Ratio.

How is this different from a simple compound return calculator?

A simple compound calculator assumes one constant annual return for a single asset. This optimizer instead tests thousands of different multi-asset weight combinations against real historical mean returns, volatility, and correlations between assets, so you can see exactly how diversification changes your risk and return — not just a single projected number.

What is the efficient frontier?

The efficient frontier is the set of portfolios that offer the highest expected return for a given level of risk. Portfolios on the frontier are efficient — you cannot get more return without taking more risk, and you cannot reduce risk without sacrificing return. The scatter chart plots every tested combination, and the upper-left edge of the cloud of dots is the efficient frontier.
